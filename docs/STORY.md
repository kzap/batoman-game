# BatoMan — Story & World Bible

The full-game plan — mechanics, music direction, palettes, and the v0.2 cut — lives in [VISION.md](VISION.md). This file is the story. Anything VISION marks as a proposal is a proposal here too, and it is not canon until Andre approves it.

## Setting

**Neo-Maynila, 2147.**

The once-chaotic streets of Manila have been transformed into a towering megacity of neon-lit skyscrapers, elevated skyways, and cramped digital slums stacked forty stories high. Jeepneys now hover on magnetic rails. Sari-sari stores sell synthetic food and black-market tech side by side. The Pasig River runs electric blue from bioluminescent algae engineered by the National Science Authority (NSA) — the government body that claims to keep the city safe, but answers to no one.

Beneath the gleaming surface, inequality festers. The wealthy live in sealed arcology towers above the smog line. Everyone else survives in the lower districts — Tondo Sublevel, Divisoria Grid, Quiapo Underground — where power cuts are frequent and NSA enforcement drones patrol day and night.

The ten districts are one walk, not ten maps. **Proposal, pending Andre:** the route follows the real city's skeleton. Tondo's docks on the bay, east into Divisoria, under Quiapo, up the outside of the NSA tower, back along the skyway toward Tondo's roofs, down the garden's pipes into the lab, along the floodgates, up the dry stair into the arcology, and out onto the same three blocks where it started, now at river level. The Pasig is under the whole walk. Level 6 is a homecoming. The raid has already happened before Level 1.

## The Hero — BatoMan

**Real name:** Andres "Ando" Batumbakal
**Age:** 19
**Origin:** Tondo Sublevel, Neo-Maynila

Ando grew up in the lower districts, the son of an urban farmer who grew vegetables in rooftop community gardens — one of the last remnants of old Manila's provincial spirit. He still wears his father's wide-brimmed **salakot** — the traditional Filipino farmer's hat — as a symbol of where he came from and what he's fighting to protect.

At 16, Ando lost his right arm in an NSA raid that leveled his neighborhood's garden district. A rogue engineer and old friend of his father illegally fitted him with an experimental **plasma buster arm** — a salvaged military-grade prosthetic originally designed as a weapons prototype. The arm looks deceptively ordinary at rest, but its forearm can reconfigure into a **plasma cannon** capable of firing rapid bursts of superheated energy.

With enough focus, Ando can channel additional power into the arm — holding the charge builds pressure until he releases a devastating **Nova Blast**: a wide-spread explosion of plasma that can shatter reinforced steel and overload mechanical systems.

He is not a trained soldier. He is a kid from the slums with a weapon arm, a farmer's hat, and a reason to fight.

The nova's fantasy is the explosion above. In the remake it currently ships as a piercing bolt (see the TDD). The brim of the salakot, in play, is the dash: Shift / X / K, with i-frames. Down is the crouch and the crouch-slide. The story can keep calling the dash a salakot dash.

### Abilities
- **Plasma Burst** — rapid-fire short-range shots, good for clearing weak enemies
- **Nova Blast** — hold to charge, release for a heavy piercing shot (the wide explosion is the design fantasy; the sim pierces until a playtest says otherwise)
- **Salakot Dash** — a committed dash with i-frames; the hat's brim is the fiction for shrugging off a hit he dashed through
- **Wall Kick** — can kick off vertical surfaces to reach higher platforms

## The Villain — Dr. Epal

**Full name:** Dr. Edilberto "Eddie" Palaganas
**Title:** Chief Science Director, National Science Authority
**Age:** 58

Dr. Epal — a nickname that stuck because of his talent for inserting himself into situations that don't concern him (*epal* is Filipino slang for someone annoyingly self-important) — was once a legitimate research scientist who genuinely believed technology could uplift the Filipino people.

That idealism curdled decades ago.

Now Dr. Epal operates as both the NSA's top scientist and its most dangerous political operator. He controls the city's infrastructure contracts, the military's weapons budget, and — secretly — a sprawling underground laboratory network beneath Neo-Maynila called **Laboratoryo Zero**. He answers directly to the Oligarch Council that funds the NSA and uses fear as his primary governance tool: when the city's population grows restless, he releases a new mechanical monster into the lower districts to remind everyone they need the NSA's protection.

He doesn't think of himself as evil. He thinks of himself as *necessary*.

He is not on screen in the four districts v2 has built. His voice arrives, if the vision is approved, on the PA inside Laboratoryo Zero, and his face arrives in the finale as the portrait ART already allows: a sealed gallery, the smile, no second health bar. Whether that is the right ending is an open question in VISION.

### Character Traits
- Brilliant but vindictive — he remembers every slight and repays them decades later
- Obsessed with legacy — wants to be remembered as the man who "modernized" the Philippines
- Publicly charming, privately cruel — beloved in NSA press releases, feared by his own staff
- Views the lower district residents as data points, not people

## The Conflict

Dr. Epal's latest project — **Project ASWANG** — is his most ambitious yet: a series of increasingly powerful mechanical monsters (loosely inspired by Filipino mythological creatures) engineered to terrorize specific districts, drive residents out, and clear land for NSA-owned arcology development.

The first ASWANG unit leveled three blocks of Tondo Sublevel — including the last operating rooftop garden in the district, the one Ando's father had rebuilt after the previous raid.

This time, Ando fights back.

That first unit is the one still standing on the docks. The build calls it the **ASWANG prototype**: unnamed, unfinished, a heavy soldier rather than a finished myth. The named units come later, once the project specializes. A concept sheet filed under Level 1 is titled Manananggal-7; the written bible and this arc put that unit in the arcology. VISION question 1 asks Andre which placement wins.

The creatures are not the villains of this story. People in the lower districts still say *tabi-tabi po*, still keep candles, still know to make noise if the moon goes dark. Project ASWANG is those stories industrialized into eviction tools. Saints and the Black Nazarene belong to the neighbourhood. They are never units.

## The arc

Four acts. Beats marked **proposal** are the glue this plan adds so the ten districts become one story. The district names, the prototype, the sigbin, the tikbalang, the kapre, the manananggal, Bakunawa, and the confrontation with Epal are canon.

### Act I — The lower city

He learns it is a project.

**1. Tondo Sublevel Docks (canon).** Pre-dawn, the prototype still hunting the piers. He puts it down. That proves a unit can fall, and it does not bring the garden back. The sari-sari shacks point him east: Divisoria is being emptied next, and the people who still write on paper are under Quiapo.

**2. Divisoria Grid Market (proposal).** Riot bots and easement barriers are pricing out the aisles. A vendor he knows puts one page of a manifest in his hand. The rest is under the chapel, because the NSA does not index candles. He leaves knowing the clearances are a list of sites. The proposed boss here is Nuno-0, the project's copy of the mound-dweller whose house you are supposed to greet rather than step on.

**3. Quiapo Underground Chapel (canon).** Candles, flooded nave, santos in the alcoves. The Sigbin Unit was sent to silence the record-keepers. **Proposal:** by candlelight he reads the list — Project ASWANG, his father's garden already marked cleared, the tower named as the eye, the lab named under the garden. The sigbin is the price of leaving with the pages.

### Act II — The way home

He climbs out, and the road bends back.

**4. NSA Tower Exterior (canon).** He climbs the outside because he was never going to be let in. The interior is logos and empty glass. There is no monster. The building is the hazard. **Proposal:** a floor plan in a window confirms the chapel's list, Laboratoryo Zero is not in this tower, and the roof is the hatch onto the skyway. An old sheet of a hangar stealth-fighter is a different idea, and this arc does not use it.

**5. Skyway Traffic (proposal).** The road the sealed city takes over everyone else. Stalled jeepneys are the platforms. It loops toward Tondo's roofs rather than away from them. From the top lane he can see the garden's dead grow-lights, and the arcology still above the smog. The proposed boss is Wakwak-4, a night-bird unit whose cry is louder when it is far.

**6. Abandoned Rooftop Garden (canon).** Rain, mutant vines, other farmers' salakot left in the planters. Tikbalang-X has done what tikbalang do: bent the paths so the people who lived here cannot find their way back. He breaks the spell for a moment at a time, in the stumble, and then for good. The paths straighten. The plants are still dead. **Proposal:** under a collapsed planter the irrigation shaft is concrete, and that shaft is the lab. Grief becomes a direction.

### Act III — Under the river

He sees the machine, and jams it.

**7. Laboratoryo Zero Entrance (canon).** Fluorescents, tanks, the files. Kapre-9 is the smoker that sat under the garden: a tree spirit built in a room with no tree. **Proposal:** Epal on the PA, charming, calling the garden contamination, and the files naming Bakunawa-Prime as the one-night finish, up the Pasig, with the floodgates as the trigger.

**8. The Floodgates (proposal).** He races the schedule and jams the gates. The river still rises enough to cut off the way back. The only dry stair climbs into the arcology, which is the address the gates were protecting. The proposed boss is Berberoka-2, the gate's heart: it drinks the channel, then vomits it back.

### Act IV — The split

**9. Arcology Atrium (canon).** White, gold, real plants, the district above the smog line. Manananggal-7 looks like authority until it tears in half. The lower body has to die first, or the flyer comes back — the folk rule, kept as the fight's rule. **Proposal:** Epal is not in the room, and a planning table has already written off the block below.

**10. Destroyed City Block (canon, ending proposed).** The three blocks from the opening, debris of every district in the rubble, the Pasig in the street. Bakunawa rises to swallow the moon; the moon-glyph is how it is hurt. The city's noise — the pans and drums people use when the sky goes dark — is the proposed way the glyph opens. Epal watches from the gallery, still necessary in his own mouth. The morning after, the NSA is still the government. One grow-light comes on in the ruined roofs. The garden is not back. It is no longer hidden.

## Tone

BatoMan is **hopeful but grounded**. It doesn't shy away from the real weight of corruption, poverty, and systemic violence — but at its core it's a story about a kid who refuses to give up on his city. There's humor in the Filipino cultural details (the jeepney drivers, the street food vendors who cheer you on, the sari-sari store owner who gives you health pickups). The world is dystopian but not joyless.

The ending this plan asks for is the same tone at city scale. He wins the night. He does not redeem the century. The hat stays on his head. One light comes back on a roof.

Story reaches the player as the level itself, plus the portraits and text ART already allows. Animated cutscenes stay out of scope until someone scopes them on purpose.

Think: *neon-soaked Neo-Manila at night, the smell of street food, bass-heavy OPM music, and a plasma cannon pointed at a robot shaped like your worst nightmare.*

- **Visual:** Blade Runner 2049 + jeepney aesthetic + Filipino street art
- **Tone:** Mega Man (gameplay heart) + Metro Manila grittiness + Filipino mythological horror
- **Music:** OPM (Original Pilipino Music) synthwave, kulintang percussion, 8-bit hybrids
